# Proverbs 17 (BSB)

← [[Proverbs 16 - BSB]] | [[Proverbs 18 - BSB]] →

**1** Better a dry morsel in quietness than a house full of feasting with strife. ^1

**2** A wise servant will rule over a disgraceful son and share his inheritance as one of the brothers. ^2

**3** A crucible for silver and a furnace for gold, but the LORD is the tester of hearts. ^3

**4** A wicked man listens to evil lips; a liar gives ear to a destructive tongue. ^4

**5** He who mocks the poor insults their Maker; whoever gloats over calamity will not go unpunished. ^5

**6** Grandchildren are the crown of the aged, and the glory of a son is his father. ^6

**7** Eloquent words are unfit for a fool; how much worse are lying lips to a ruler! ^7

**8** A bribe is a charm to its giver; wherever he turns, he succeeds. ^8

**9** Whoever conceals an offense promotes love, but he who brings it up separates friends. ^9

**10** A rebuke cuts into a man of discernment deeper than a hundred lashes cut into a fool. ^10

**11** An evil man seeks only rebellion; a cruel messenger will be sent against him. ^11

**12** It is better to meet a bear robbed of her cubs than a fool in his folly. ^12

**13** If anyone returns evil for good, evil will never leave his house. ^13

**14** To start a quarrel is to release a flood; so abandon the dispute before it breaks out. ^14

**15** Acquitting the guilty and condemning the righteous—both are detestable to the LORD. ^15

**16** Why should the fool have money in his hand with no intention of buying wisdom? ^16

**17** A friend loves at all times, and a brother is born for adversity. ^17

**18** A man lacking judgment strikes hands in pledge and puts up security for his neighbor. ^18

**19** He who loves transgression loves strife; he who builds his gate high invites destruction. ^19

**20** The one with a perverse heart finds no good, and he whose tongue is deceitful falls into trouble. ^20

**21** A man fathers a fool to his own grief; the father of a fool has no joy. ^21

**22** A joyful heart is good medicine, but a broken spirit dries up the bones. ^22

**23** A wicked man takes a covert bribe to subvert the course of justice. ^23

**24** Wisdom is the focus of the discerning, but the eyes of a fool wander to the ends of the earth. ^24

**25** A foolish son brings grief to his father and bitterness to her who bore him. ^25

**26** It is surely not good to punish the innocent or to flog a noble for his honesty. ^26

**27** A man of knowledge restrains his words, and a man of understanding maintains a calm spirit. ^27

**28** Even a fool is considered wise if he keeps silent, and discerning when he holds his tongue. ^28
