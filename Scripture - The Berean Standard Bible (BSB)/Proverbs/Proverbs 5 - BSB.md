# Proverbs 5 (BSB)

← [[Proverbs 4 - BSB]] | [[Proverbs 6 - BSB]] →

**1** My son, pay attention to my wisdom; incline your ear to my insight, ^1

**2** that you may maintain discretion and your lips may preserve knowledge. ^2

**3** Though the lips of the forbidden woman drip honey and her speech is smoother than oil, ^3

**4** in the end she is bitter as wormwood, sharp as a double-edged sword. ^4

**5** Her feet go down to death; her steps lead straight to Sheol. ^5

**6** She does not consider the path of life; she does not know that her ways are unstable. ^6

**7** So now, my sons, listen to me, and do not turn aside from the words of my mouth. ^7

**8** Keep your path far from her; do not go near the door of her house, ^8

**9** lest you concede your vigor to others, and your years to one who is cruel; ^9

**10** lest strangers feast on your wealth, and your labors enrich the house of a foreigner. ^10

**11** At the end of your life you will groan when your flesh and your body are spent, ^11

**12** and you will say, “How I hated discipline, and my heart despised reproof! ^12

**13** I did not listen to the voice of my teachers or incline my ear to my mentors. ^13

**14** I am on the brink of utter ruin in the midst of the whole assembly.” ^14

**15** Drink water from your own cistern, and running water from your own well. ^15

**16** Why should your springs flow in the streets, your streams of water in the public squares? ^16

**17** Let them be yours alone, never to be shared with strangers. ^17

**18** May your fountain be blessed, and may you rejoice in the wife of your youth: ^18

**19** A loving doe, a graceful fawn—may her breasts satisfy you always; may you be captivated by her love forever. ^19

**20** Why be captivated, my son, by an adulteress, or embrace the bosom of a stranger? ^20

**21** For a man’s ways are before the eyes of the LORD, and the LORD examines all his paths. ^21

**22** The iniquities of a wicked man entrap him; the cords of his sin entangle him. ^22

**23** He dies for lack of discipline, led astray by his own great folly. ^23
