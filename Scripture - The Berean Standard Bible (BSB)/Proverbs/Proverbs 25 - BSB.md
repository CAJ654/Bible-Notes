# Proverbs 25 (BSB)

← [[Proverbs 24 - BSB]] | [[Proverbs 26 - BSB]] →

**1** These are additional proverbs of Solomon, which were copied by the men of Hezekiah king of Judah: ^1

**2** It is the glory of God to conceal a matter and the glory of kings to search it out. ^2

**3** As the heavens are high and the earth is deep, so the hearts of kings cannot be searched. ^3

**4** Remove the dross from the silver, and a vessel for a silversmith will come forth. ^4

**5** Remove the wicked from the king’s presence, and his throne will be established in righteousness. ^5

**6** Do not exalt yourself in the presence of the king, and do not stand in the place of great men; ^6

**7** for it is better to be told, “Come up here!” than to be demoted in the presence of the prince. Even what you have seen with your own eyes, ^7

**8** do not bring hastily to court. Otherwise, what will you do in the end when your neighbor puts you to shame? ^8

**9** Argue your case with your neighbor without betraying another’s confidence, ^9

**10** lest he who hears you bring shame upon you, and your infamy never go away. ^10

**11** A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in settings of silver. ^11

**12** Like an earring of gold or an ornament of fine gold is a wise man’s rebuke to a listening ear. ^12

**13** Like the cold of snow in the time of harvest is a trustworthy messenger to those who send him; he refreshes the soul of his masters. ^13

**14** Like clouds and wind without rain is the man who boasts of gifts never given. ^14

**15** Through patience a ruler can be persuaded, and a gentle tongue can break a bone. ^15

**16** If you find honey, eat just what you need, lest you have too much and vomit it up. ^16

**17** Seldom set foot in your neighbor’s house, lest he grow weary and hate you. ^17

**18** Like a club or sword or sharp arrow is a man who bears false witness against his neighbor. ^18

**19** Like a broken tooth or a foot out of joint is confidence in a faithless man in time of trouble. ^19

**20** Like one who removes a garment on a cold day or vinegar poured on a wound is one who sings songs to a heavy heart. ^20

**21** If your enemy is hungry, give him food to eat, and if he is thirsty, give him water to drink. ^21

**22** For in so doing, you will heap burning coals on his head, and the LORD will reward you. ^22

**23** As the north wind brings forth rain, so a backbiting tongue brings angry looks. ^23

**24** Better to live on a corner of the roof than to share a house with a quarrelsome wife. ^24

**25** Like cold water to a weary soul is good news from a distant land. ^25

**26** Like a muddied spring or a polluted well is a righteous man who gives way to the wicked. ^26

**27** It is not good to eat too much honey or to search out one’s own glory. ^27

**28** Like a city whose walls are broken down is a man who does not control his temper. ^28
