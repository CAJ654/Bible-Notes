# Job 41 (BSB)

← [[Job 40 - BSB]] | [[Job 42 - BSB]] →

**1** “Can you pull in Leviathan with a hook or tie down his tongue with a rope? ^1

**2** Can you put a cord through his nose or pierce his jaw with a hook? ^2

**3** Will he beg you for mercy or speak to you softly? ^3

**4** Will he make a covenant with you to take him as a slave for life? ^4

**5** Can you pet him like a bird or put him on a leash for your maidens? ^5

**6** Will traders barter for him or divide him among the merchants? ^6

**7** Can you fill his hide with harpoons or his head with fishing spears? ^7

**8** If you lay a hand on him, you will remember the battle and never repeat it! ^8

**9** Surely hope of overcoming him is false. Is not the sight of him overwhelming? ^9

**10** No one is so fierce as to rouse Leviathan. Then who is able to stand against Me? ^10

**11** Who has given to Me that I should repay him? Everything under heaven is Mine. ^11

**12** I cannot keep silent about his limbs, his power and graceful form. ^12

**13** Who can strip off his outer coat? Who can approach him with a bridle? ^13

**14** Who can open his jaws, ringed by his fearsome teeth? ^14

**15** His rows of scales are his pride, tightly sealed together. ^15

**16** One scale is so near to another that no air can pass between them. ^16

**17** They are joined to one another; they clasp and cannot be separated. ^17

**18** His snorting flashes with light, and his eyes are like the rays of dawn. ^18

**19** Firebrands stream from his mouth; fiery sparks shoot forth! ^19

**20** Smoke billows from his nostrils as from a boiling pot over burning reeds. ^20

**21** His breath sets coals ablaze, and flames pour from his mouth. ^21

**22** Strength resides in his neck, and dismay leaps before him. ^22

**23** The folds of his flesh are tightly joined; they are firm and immovable. ^23

**24** His chest is as hard as a rock, as hard as a lower millstone! ^24

**25** When Leviathan rises up, the mighty are terrified; they withdraw before his thrashing. ^25

**26** The sword that reaches him has no effect, nor does the spear or dart or arrow. ^26

**27** He regards iron as straw and bronze as rotten wood. ^27

**28** No arrow can make him flee; slingstones become like chaff to him. ^28

**29** A club is regarded as straw, and he laughs at the sound of the lance. ^29

**30** His undersides are jagged potsherds, spreading out the mud like a threshing sledge. ^30

**31** He makes the depths seethe like a cauldron; he makes the sea like a jar of ointment. ^31

**32** He leaves a glistening wake behind him; one would think the deep had white hair! ^32

**33** Nothing on earth is his equal—a creature devoid of fear! ^33

**34** He looks down on all the haughty; he is king over all the proud.” ^34
