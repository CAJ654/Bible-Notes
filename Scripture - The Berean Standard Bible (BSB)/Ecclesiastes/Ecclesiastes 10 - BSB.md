# Ecclesiastes 10 (BSB)

← [[Ecclesiastes 9 - BSB]] | [[Ecclesiastes 11 - BSB]] →

**1** As dead flies bring a stench to the perfumer’s oil, so a little folly outweighs wisdom and honor. ^1

**2** A wise man’s heart inclines to the right, but the heart of a fool to the left. ^2

**3** Even as the fool walks along the road, his sense is lacking, and he shows everyone that he is a fool. ^3

**4** If the ruler’s temper flares against you, do not abandon your post, for calmness lays great offenses to rest. ^4

**5** There is an evil I have seen under the sun—an error that proceeds from the ruler: ^5

**6** Folly is appointed to great heights, but the rich sit in lowly positions. ^6

**7** I have seen slaves on horseback, while princes go on foot like slaves. ^7

**8** He who digs a pit may fall into it, and he who breaches a wall may be bitten by a snake. ^8

**9** The one who quarries stones may be injured by them, and he who splits logs endangers himself. ^9

**10** If the axe is dull and the blade unsharpened, more strength must be exerted, but skill produces success. ^10

**11** If the snake bites before it is charmed, there is no profit for the charmer. ^11

**12** The words of a wise man’s mouth are gracious, but the lips of a fool consume him. ^12

**13** The beginning of his talk is folly, and the end of his speech is evil madness. ^13

**14** Yet the fool multiplies words. No one knows what is coming, and who can tell him what will come after him? ^14

**15** The toil of a fool wearies him, for he does not know the way to the city. ^15

**16** Woe to you, O land whose king is a youth, and whose princes feast in the morning. ^16

**17** Blessed are you, O land whose king is a son of nobles, and whose princes feast at the proper time—for strength and not for drunkenness. ^17

**18** Through laziness the roof caves in, and in the hands of the idle, the house leaks. ^18

**19** A feast is prepared for laughter, and wine makes life merry, but money is the answer for everything. ^19

**20** Do not curse the king even in your thoughts, or curse the rich even in your bedroom, for a bird of the air may carry your words, and a winged creature may report your speech. ^20
