# Proverbs 5 (WMB)

← [[Proverbs 4 - WMB]] | [[Proverbs 6 - WMB]] →

**1** My son, pay attention to my wisdom. Turn your ear to my understanding, ^1

**2** that you may maintain discretion, that your lips may preserve knowledge. ^2

**3** For the lips of an adulteress drip honey. Her mouth is smoother than oil, ^3

**4** but in the end she is as bitter as wormwood, and as sharp as a two-edged sword. ^4

**5** Her feet go down to death. Her steps lead straight to Sheol. ^5

**6** She gives no thought to the way of life. Her ways are crooked, and she doesn’t know it. ^6

**7** Now therefore, my sons, listen to me. Don’t depart from the words of my mouth. ^7

**8** Remove your way far from her. Don’t come near the door of her house, ^8

**9** lest you give your honor to others, and your years to the cruel one; ^9

**10** lest strangers feast on your wealth, and your labors enrich another man’s house. ^10

**11** You will groan at your latter end, when your flesh and your body are consumed, ^11

**12** and say, “How I have hated instruction, and my heart despised reproof. ^12

**13** I haven’t obeyed the voice of my teachers, nor turned my ear to those who instructed me! ^13

**14** I have come to the brink of utter ruin, among the gathered assembly.” ^14

**15** Drink water out of your own cistern, running water out of your own well. ^15

**16** Should your springs overflow in the streets, streams of water in the public squares? ^16

**17** Let them be for yourself alone, not for strangers with you. ^17

**18** Let your spring be blessed. Rejoice in the wife of your youth. ^18

**19** A loving doe and a graceful deer— let her breasts satisfy you at all times. Be captivated always with her love. ^19

**20** For why should you, my son, be captivated with an adulteress? Why embrace the bosom of another? ^20

**21** For the ways of man are before the LORD’s eyes. He examines all his paths. ^21

**22** The evil deeds of the wicked ensnare him. The cords of his sin hold him firmly. ^22

**23** He will die for lack of instruction. In the greatness of his folly, he will go astray. ^23
