# Job 41 (WMB)

← [[Job 40 - WMB]] | [[Job 42 - WMB]] →

**1** “Can you draw out Leviathan with a fish hook, or press down his tongue with a cord? ^1

**2** Can you put a rope into his nose, or pierce his jaw through with a hook? ^2

**3** Will he make many petitions to you, or will he speak soft words to you? ^3

**4** Will he make a covenant with you, that you should take him for a servant forever? ^4

**5** Will you play with him as with a bird? Or will you bind him for your girls? ^5

**6** Will traders barter for him? Will they part him among the merchants? ^6

**7** Can you fill his skin with barbed irons, or his head with fish spears? ^7

**8** Lay your hand on him. Remember the battle, and do so no more. ^8

**9** Behold, the hope of him is in vain. Won’t one be cast down even at the sight of him? ^9

**10** None is so fierce that he dare stir him up. Who then is he who can stand before me? ^10

**11** Who has first given to me, that I should repay him? Everything under the heavens is mine. ^11

**12** “I will not keep silence concerning his limbs, nor his mighty strength, nor his goodly frame. ^12

**13** Who can strip off his outer garment? Who will come within his jaws? ^13

**14** Who can open the doors of his face? Around his teeth is terror. ^14

**15** Strong scales are his pride, shut up together with a close seal. ^15

**16** One is so near to another, that no air can come between them. ^16

**17** They are joined to one another. They stick together, so that they can’t be pulled apart. ^17

**18** His sneezing flashes out light. His eyes are like the eyelids of the morning. ^18

**19** Out of his mouth go burning torches. Sparks of fire leap out. ^19

**20** Out of his nostrils a smoke goes, as of a boiling pot over a fire of reeds. ^20

**21** His breath kindles coals. A flame goes out of his mouth. ^21

**22** There is strength in his neck. Terror dances before him. ^22

**23** The flakes of his flesh are joined together. They are firm on him. They can’t be moved. ^23

**24** His heart is as firm as a stone, yes, firm as the lower millstone. ^24

**25** When he raises himself up, the mighty are afraid. They retreat before his thrashing. ^25

**26** If one attacks him with the sword, it can’t prevail; nor the spear, the dart, nor the pointed shaft. ^26

**27** He counts iron as straw, and bronze as rotten wood. ^27

**28** The arrow can’t make him flee. Sling stones are like chaff to him. ^28

**29** Clubs are counted as stubble. He laughs at the rushing of the javelin. ^29

**30** His undersides are like sharp potsherds, leaving a trail in the mud like a threshing sledge. ^30

**31** He makes the deep to boil like a pot. He makes the sea like a pot of ointment. ^31

**32** He makes a path shine after him. One would think the deep had white hair. ^32

**33** On earth there is not his equal, that is made without fear. ^33

**34** He sees everything that is high. He is king over all the sons of pride.” ^34
