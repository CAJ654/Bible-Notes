# 4 Maccabees (Apocrypha) 11 (WEBU)

← [[4 Maccabees (Apocrypha) 10 - WEBU]] | [[4 Maccabees (Apocrypha) 12 - WEBU]] →

**1** When he had died, disfigured in his torments, the fifth leaped forward, and said, ^1

**2** “I don’t intend, O tyrant, to get excused from the torment which is on behalf of virtue. ^2

**3** But I have come of my own accord, that by my death you may owe heavenly vengeance and punishment for more crimes. ^3

**4** O you hater of virtue and of men, what have we done that you thus revel in our blood? ^4

**5** Does it seem evil to you that we worship the Founder of all things, and live according to his surpassing law? ^5

**6** But this is worthy of honors, not torments, ^6

**7** if you had been capable of the higher feelings of men, and possessed the hope of salvation from God. ^7

**8** Behold now, being alien from God, you make war against those who are religious toward God.” ^8

**9** As he said this, the spearbearers bound him and drew him to the rack, ^9

**10** to which binding him at his knees, and fastening them with iron fetters, they bent down his loins upon the wedge of the wheel; and his body was then dismembered, scorpion-fashion. ^10

**11** With his breath thus confined, and his body strangled, he said, ^11

**12** “A great favor you bestow upon us, O tyrant, by enabling us to manifest our adherence to the law by means of nobler sufferings.” ^12

**13** He also being dead, the sixth, quite a youth, was brought out. On the tyrant asking him whether he would eat and be delivered, he said, ^13

**14** “I am indeed younger than my brothers, but in understanding I am as old. ^14

**15** I was born and reared to the same end. We are bound to die also on behalf of the same cause. ^15

**16** So if you think it is proper to torment us for not eating the unclean, then torment!” ^16

**17** As he said this, they brought him to the wheel. ^17

**18** Extended upon this, with limbs racked and dislocated, he was gradually roasted from beneath. ^18

**19** Having heated sharp spits, they stabbed them into his back; and having pierced his sides, they burned away his entrails. ^19

**20** He, while tormented, said, “O good and holy contest, in which for the sake of religion, we kindred have been called to the arena of pain, and have not been conquered. ^20

**21** For religious understanding, O tyrant, is unconquered. ^21

**22** Armed with upright virtue, I also will depart with my kindred. ^22

**23** I, too, bearing with me a great avenger, O inventor of tortures, and enemy of the truly pious. ^23

**24** We six youths have destroyed your tyranny. ^24

**25** For isn’t your inability to overrule our reasoning, and to compel us to eat the unclean, your destruction? ^25

**26** Your fire is cold to us. Your racks are painless, and your violence harmless. ^26

**27** For the guards not of a tyrant but of a divine law are our defenders. Through this we keep our reasoning unconquered.” ^27
