# Proverbs 27 (WEBU)

← [[Proverbs 26 - WEBU]] | [[Proverbs 28 - WEBU]] →

**1** Don’t boast about tomorrow; for you don’t know what a day may bring. ^1

**2** Let another man praise you, and not your own mouth; a stranger, and not your own lips. ^2

**3** A stone is heavy, and sand is a burden; but a fool’s provocation is heavier than both. ^3

**4** Wrath is cruel, and anger is overwhelming; but who is able to stand before jealousy? ^4

**5** Better is open rebuke than hidden love. ^5

**6** The wounds of a friend are faithful, although the kisses of an enemy are profuse. ^6

**7** A full soul loathes a honeycomb; but to a hungry soul, every bitter thing is sweet. ^7

**8** As a bird that wanders from her nest, so is a man who wanders from his home. ^8

**9** Perfume and incense bring joy to the heart; so does earnest counsel from a man’s friend. ^9

**10** Don’t forsake your friend and your father’s friend. Don’t go to your brother’s house in the day of your disaster. A neighbor who is near is better than a distant brother. ^10

**11** Be wise, my son, and bring joy to my heart, then I can answer my tormentor. ^11

**12** A prudent man sees danger and takes refuge; but the simple pass on, and suffer for it. ^12

**13** Take his garment when he puts up collateral for a stranger. Hold it for a wayward woman! ^13

**14** He who blesses his neighbor with a loud voice early in the morning, it will be taken as a curse by him. ^14

**15** A continual dropping on a rainy day and a contentious wife are alike: ^15

**16** restraining her is like restraining the wind, or like grasping oil in his right hand. ^16

**17** Iron sharpens iron; so a man sharpens his friend’s countenance. ^17

**18** Whoever tends the fig tree shall eat its fruit. He who looks after his master shall be honored. ^18

**19** Like water reflects a face, so a man’s heart reflects the man. ^19

**20** Sheol and Abaddon are never satisfied; and a man’s eyes are never satisfied. ^20

**21** The crucible is for silver, and the furnace for gold; but man is refined by his praise. ^21

**22** Though you grind a fool in a mortar with a pestle along with grain, yet his foolishness will not be removed from him. ^22

**23** Know well the state of your flocks, and pay attention to your herds, ^23

**24** for riches are not forever, nor does the crown endure to all generations. ^24

**25** The hay is removed, and the new growth appears, the grasses of the hills are gathered in. ^25

**26** The lambs are for your clothing, and the goats are the price of a field. ^26

**27** There will be plenty of goats’ milk for your food, for your family’s food, and for the nourishment of your servant girls. ^27
